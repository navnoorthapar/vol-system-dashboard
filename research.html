No option prices exist in the window, so every straddle is priced and marked with Black–Scholes at β·VIX (β = 0.82). Sizing is premium as a share of the sleeve’s NAV; a short straddle is capped at NAV / (20% × K × 100) contracts, its initial margin; “overlay”: the portfolio sleeves’ size is then scaled by their pairwise P&L correlation and trailing net Sharpe, lagged one session. Costs are charged at entry and at exit (vol pts of the straddle’s vega); the cash account and the hedge carry r and q. z_vrp: the 252-session z-score of VRP = VIX − RV₂₀ (annualised), the day's premium of implied over realised vol. PDV forecast: the walk-forward 21-session RMS vol forecast; the spread is the forecast minus VIX. Regimes are the rule labels lagged one session. A position still on when its 30-day straddle expires is rolled into a fresh one the same close (“at expiry, rolled”). The trade log also holds 111 “combined” records (108 long, 3 short): the research-only agreement rule over S1C, S3 and S4, which reads S3 as long index vol, the opposite of the short straddle the backtest trades. It is not a coherent strategy, is not in the portfolio and is left out here. S3_PROXY_COST (0.1% of notional) costs only the pre-C22 z-score proxy the C22 bridge keeps; the canonical S3 is costed as a straddle. Every number in the table is read from joint_vol_calibration/signals/signal_engine.py and joint_vol_calibration/backtest/backtest_engine.py when the page is built, and the counts from the trade log in data_store/backtest/full_results.pkl.

Why the edge disappeared

Priced at β = 0.82 × VIX, S1 is −$307K (58% win), and its sign flips between β = 0.85 and 0.90. The contrarian inversion S1C is −$454K, so neither direction of the PDV-spread trade makes money. The portfolio is −22.2% cumulative (Sharpe −0.99, max drawdown −22.2%); S3 (+$61K, 49% win) is the only positive sleeve, positive for β ≥ 0.80 in the sensitivity grid, negative at 0.78; like every sleeve’s, its P&L is an excess return over T-bills (the hurdle is 0). Each sleeve’s sign at each β is in the β grid; what the β·VIX mark leaves of the premium, against realised vol, is in implied against realised.

Sleeve notes, stress windows and the C18 inversion

S3 short SPX straddle (index leg of a dispersion trade; no single-name leg) fires only at VIX/VVIX extremes: 39 trades in 2018–2025 (26 entries plus 13 rolls of a position held past its straddle's expiry), 49% win rate. Since C22 it is priced as the index leg of the dispersion trade (a short SPX straddle; there is no single-stock options data), and its entry timing does not beat 3,550 exposure-matched circular shifts at the 5% level (p = 0.078; Holm over 6 sleeves 0.47; 2,000 trade shuffles p = 0.057).

S1 PDV (ref), the PDV short-VRP reference strategy: 69 of 69 trades are short straddles sold in R1; worst trade −$183K (2018-01-09 → 2018-02-07). Selling straddles at the VIX books the skew premium as profit.

S1C contrarian flipped S1's direction on the premise that S1 lost both ways; C18 showed that premise was the bug. It stays a sleeve because the portfolio's composition is held fixed across correction rounds so they remain comparable. S4 VRP enters on z_vrp declining from a peak; its P&L on 2018–2025 is −$274K.

Stress windows, each measured from the close before it opens: COVID 2020 (2020-02-19 → 2020-04-30) −0.2%; FedHikes 2022 (2022-01-01 → 2022-12-31) +4.1%; Tariffs 2025 (2025-01-01 → 2025-03-24) −1.1%. The backtest ends 2025-03-24, so the April 2025 tariff shock is not in it, and this is no evidence of resilience to it.

The C16–C23 corrections, each with its root cause, fix, tests and effect, are on the Corrections page.

Limitations

  • Option prices. All P&L is Black–Scholes on β·VIX; the database's 9 SPX chains (92,556 rows) are from 2026-03-24 to 2026-10-29.
  • Breadth. S3 enters 26 times in 2018–2025.
  • Execution. Costs are a flat assumption; no quote-level bid–ask in the window.
