Inside a rough-volatility model the skew scales as T^(H−1/2); this is a descriptive slope of one fitted surface per session, sensitive to the shortest expiries and to the parametrisation, not an estimate of H (the rough-Heston H sits on its 0.02 floor on 2026-11-05: rough vs Heston). 16 stored sessions: a range across sessions, not a sampling interval.

How the skews are computed
  • SVI-cal (the Surface page’s fitted slices): the analytic slope of each fitted slice at the forward, w′(0) / (2·√(w(0)·T)), with w′(k) = b(ρ + (k − m)/√((k − m)² + σ²)).
  • eSSVI: the surface report’s stored ATM skew (term_structure.atm_skew_essvi), 2026-11-05 only. eSSVI’s ψ(θ) shapes the skew across expiries, so its short end differs from SVI-cal’s; both are shown, neither is preferred.
  • Heston: central differences of the model smile at h = 0.01 and 0.005 in ln(K/F), Richardson-extrapolated, at 2026-11-05’s listed maturities: the published parameters (κ 8.69, σ 2.00) and the σ-profile refit with σ ≤ 10 (κ 35.94, σ 10.00; calibrations/diagnostics/2026-11-05.json).
  • Skews are negative (puts over calls); the chart plots magnitudes on log axes, so a straight line is a power law.
