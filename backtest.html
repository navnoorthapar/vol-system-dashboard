C18 — a strike-rolling bug contaminated every earlier number

The straddle engine re-entered a position the same day it exited, so the position series never returned to flat: a continuously held position was one trade whose strike was fixed at first entry and whose tenor decayed to its 1-day floor, instead of a fresh 30-day ATM straddle rolled at every max-hold. The book held a single stale-strike, perpetually expiring straddle — a directional |S − K| bet, not a vol trade — and this contaminated all P&L from C10 to C17. Fixing it (forcing a flat day so straddles roll) inverted the contrarian thesis: the “catastrophic” S1 (−$1.53M) that motivated flipping its direction into S1C was the artifact.

After C18, S1 looked like a profitable short-VRP harvester (+$694K by C21). C22 showed that profit was the skew premium inside the VIX: straddles had been priced at the VIX, a 30-day variance-swap rate, not at ATM vol. Priced at β = 0.82 × VIX, S1 is −$288K (59% win); its sign flips between β = 0.85 and 0.90. The contrarian inversion S1C is −$431K, so neither direction of the PDV-spread trade makes money. The portfolio is −21.6% cumulative (Sharpe −0.88, max drawdown −21.6%); S3 (+$56K, 49% win) is the only positive sleeve, positive for β ≥ 0.80 in the sensitivity grid, negative at 0.78, and below the T-bill return.

S3 · dispersion (index leg) fires only at VIX/VVIX extremes: 39 trades in 2018–2025 (26 entries plus 13 rolls of a position held past its straddle's expiry), 49% win rate. Since C22 it is priced as the index leg of the dispersion trade (a short SPX straddle; there is no single-stock options data), and its timing, tested on S3 at base (pass-1) size, before the sizing overlay, does not beat random entry dates at the 5% level (p = 0.075). The pre-C22 “+$23K at every z-scale” was not option P&L.

Stress windows, each measured from the close before it opens: COVID 2020 (2020-02-19 → 2020-04-30) +1.7%; FedHikes 2022 (2022-01-01 → 2022-12-31) +4.7%; Tariffs 2025 (2025-01-01 → 2025-03-24) −1.1% — they cannot pay for the bleed between them. The backtest ends 2025-03-24, so the 2025 window covers only the run-up: the April 2025 tariff shock is not in it, and this is no evidence of resilience to it.

C16–C22 Methodology Audit — 19 Flaws Found, 17 Fixed, 2 Quantified

Self-audit of the methodology (C16: 2026-06-11, a C17 red-team the same week, C18–C22 after). Every number on this page is from the C22 run (β = 0.82, one adaptive R2 gate, rf = 0 Sharpe). Each successive fix made the backtest worse: the original “edge” was a stack of artifacts. The Evidence column gives the effect each correction had when it was made; for C22 it is read from the stored correction bridge, which applies the fixes one at a time.

After removing every identified artifact — including the strike-rolling bug that had been distorting the P&L the whole time — the portfolio is −21.6% and the only positive sleeve is S3's index-leg proxy, which depends on β. S1, the former short-VRP “harvester”, is −$288K once straddles are priced at ATM vol rather than the VIX — not deployable. The deliverable is the infrastructure (calibration, Greeks, hedging, walk-forward harness) and the audit trail; the trading signals are the case study in how backtests flatter themselves.

Regime-exit variants · S1/S2 research

not simulated

The R2-exit variants (S1X, S2X, S1RF) are not part of the stored backtest, so no figures are shown. The table previously on this page approximated them by zeroing P&L on R2 days instead of simulating the exit (closing the straddle, paying exit costs, forgoing later P&L, re-entering at a new strike); that approximation got the sign of the S2 effect wrong.

Limitations and what would lift them

Option prices. All P&L is Black–Scholes on β·VIX — the database holds no option prices inside the backtest window (its 5 SPX chains, 68,006 rows, are snapshots from 2026-03-24 to 2026-10-16; β comes from 4 of them plus a live capture, and 1 is a later check). Historical end-of-day chains would turn the backtest from an estimate into evidence.

Jumps. The one Bates SVJ fit did not converge (λ ended at its bound and the fit was worse than nested Heston), and daily returns cannot separate frequent small jumps from rare large ones; intraday data (bipower variation) would.

Breadth. S3 enters 26 times in 2018–2025; by the fundamental law of active management that breadth cannot support an information ratio. Single-name option data across index constituents would.

Execution. Costs are a flat assumption; quote-level bid–ask data would test whether a thin vol-carry edge survives the round trip. The Monte-Carlo-priced Quintic OU calibration is slow (see the calibration page).
