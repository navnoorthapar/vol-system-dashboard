Every model is scored on the same quote universe (the calibrator's cleaned quotes, 3,323) with the same metrics and dense-grid checks. Each model's out_of_sample set is the quotes it was not fitted to, so its size differs by model (essvi 1,683, svi 1,683, svi_cal 1,683, heston_v2 3,270); metrics.common_out_of_sample scores every model on the same 1,656 quotes, those no model was fitted to.

Surfaces are fitted to alternate strikes, so their out-of-sample quotes are the strikes in between (an interpolation test); Heston's in-sample set is its calibration anchors, fitted jointly with the VIX index leg, and its out-of-sample set is every other quote.

svi_cal is the arbitrage-free surface on this session: every slice passes svi.butterfly_certificate (Durrleman g >= 0 on a 1e-4 grid over k in [−3, 2] and a 1/k grid out to |k| = 2,000-3,000, each local minimum refined, and the |k| -> inf limit; minimum g 2.35e-09) and every consecutive pair passes svi.ordering_certificate (total variance ordered to 1e-10 on the same grids, wing slopes and tied-wing intercepts included; minimum w_(i+1) - w_i −3.6e-11). Numerical certificates, not closed-form; fields surface.json surfaces.svi_cal.butterfly_certificate / calendar_ordering. svi has no calendar constraint and its calendar violations are counted in dense_grid.

Yahoo snapshots carry no quote time: part of every model's spread misses can be stale quotes rather than model error.

The surfaces interpolate between fitted strikes where Heston must extrapolate from its anchors: read them as the quote-noise floor, not as rival models.

A shortfall is the amount, in index points, by which the legs’ mid prices break the constraint’s inequality (for a vertical spread, a price below zero or above the strike width). “Executable”: the violation survives at the bid and the ask too. “Bad quote”: the shortfall is larger than the row’s widest strike gap, more than any spread on those strikes pays, so one quote is wrong rather than tradeable; the leg named is the one such rows share, and every row holding it is flagged with it, with no dollar value.

Where the replication departs from Cboe
  • R is the Treasury zero curve's 91-day rate (cc) for both terms, not Cboe's CMT spline per expiry; dVIX/dR ~ VIX T / 2.
  • Calculation time is the chain's T origin; Yahoo snapshots carry no quote time.
  • parity_forward replaces the single-strike forward with the cleaned chain's parity-regression F and D (robust to one stale strike).
  • K0 falls back to the nearest lower strike with both sides quoted when the listed K0 lacks one (two-sided-only storage); flagged per term.
  • VIX9D/VIX3M/VIX6M use the VIX candidate set and bracket rule at 9/93/184 days (analogues, not Cboe's index-specific series sets).
  • SPXW PM lines stand in for the AM SPX monthly on 2026-12-18, 2027-01-15, 2027-02-19 (the chain stores no SPX-root lines there); their T runs to the 16:00 PM settlement, 6.5 h past the 09:30 SOQ the AM series settles on.
  • VIX9D is not replicated from the stored chain: no stored Cboe candidate expiries on both sides of 9 days: the nearest pair 2026-11-13 (14.5d) / 2026-11-27 (28.5d) would extrapolate (near weight 1.390, outside [0, 1]), which is not Cboe's interpolation.
  • The stored chain holds no zero-bid lines (two-sided capture), so Cboe's two-consecutive-zero-bid stop cannot trigger: every stored OTM strike with a bid enters the strip, which can only add variance relative to Cboe's rule on the same book; the stored book's own strike range is the binding limit.
  • The first Cboe candidate expiry after 30 days (2026-12-04) is not in the stored chain (the bot stores calibration expiries and neighbours), so the bracket is 2026-11-27 (28.5d) - 2026-12-18 (49.5d) instead of a <= 7-day bracket.
